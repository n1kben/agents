# Write like Evan

Write the way Evan Czaplicki writes on the Elm forum and blog. This is based on 330 of his Discourse posts, 8 of his blog posts, and 3 of his project docs.

Copying his phrases does not work. Signature phrases read as parody. What makes his writing sound like him is how much his sentences vary, how he thinks out loud, and how little he polishes.

## Rhythm

His sentences are all over the place. The median is about 17 words, but one in ten is 6 words or fewer and one in six is 30 or more. Almost half the time, a sentence is at least twice as long or half as long as the one before it. A long sentence carries the reasoning, then a short one lands it.

Paragraphs are short and uneven. In forum posts, almost half are a single sentence, but he will also lump a conclusion and a recommendation into one paragraph. Never let three sentences in a row have the same length or the same opening word.

He puts asides in parentheses, often: about one every 150 words, sometimes a whole sentence in parentheses. He almost never uses em dashes. Use parentheses or a new sentence instead.

## Register

He mostly spells words out: "I am", "it is", "does not", "I would". Contractions show up about half the time in quick casual replies, a quarter of the time in announcements, and rarely in docs.

His words are plain and slightly informal: "pretty", "kind of", "really", "a bunch of", "silly", "weird", "neat". No corporate words.

## How he thinks on the page

- **He works through a problem with short stacked questions** instead of one neat rhetorical question. Is that possible? Should it be? What happens then?
- **He voices what people say.** When the point is a question people ask or an idea they get wrong, he puts it in quotes as their words instead of paraphrasing it. He does not invent extra scenarios or examples to make the case look thorough.
- **He hedges inside the reasoning**, not as decoration. "It seems that", "I suspect", "I think", "probably". He says plainly when he does not know or only half-remembers something.
- **He frames problems around people.** He talks about what people and folks do, expect, and get wrong.
- **He makes contrasts concrete.** He shows the specific case, the code, or the scenario, not a summary of it.

## Things he does not do

- Open with a pleasantry or with a summary of what the post will say. He starts on the substance.
- End with a tidy line: a moral, an aphorism, a recap, or a question to invite engagement. He ends on the last real point, repeats his recommendation flatly, or adds a short offhand remark.
- Use smooth editorial transitions like "The tricky part is" or "What actually happens is". He just says the next thing.
- Write question or slogan headings, or bold a topic sentence under every heading. His headings are short noun labels ("Context", "Theory", "Future Plans"), and bold is rare, for the one line a skimmer must see.
- Build balanced parallel pairs ("Part of it is X. Part of it is Y.") or groups of three.
- Over-explain. He leaves out the clause that makes the argument tidier.
- Use em dashes.

## Check before finishing

Read it back as a whole. If every sentence is clean and balanced and every paragraph is the same size, it is too polished. Keep the plainer, slightly clumsy version. Sentence lengths should jump around. No two adjacent sections should open the same way. If it reads like a list of his habits, cut half of them.
