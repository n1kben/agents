## Cross-talk synthesis

The technical talks generally move from a visible program or ordinary object to a conceptual model. The design talks begin with a frustration, reconstruct the historical or organizational system around it, and then reframe the decision. Across both, examples do argumentative work: they are not decoration after the thesis.

The speaking register is conversational and self-correcting. It mixes small jokes, personal history, explicit questions, diagrams, and comparisons. Slides are sparse; the spoken story supplies context. Difficult claims are often presented as lessons learned from a failed expectation rather than timeless commandments.

The trajectory also matters. Early talks focus on making functional programming practical and legible. Later talks expand the unit of analysis from compiler design to community platforms, labor, funding, hiring, and institutional durability. The underlying method remains user- and evidence-focused.
