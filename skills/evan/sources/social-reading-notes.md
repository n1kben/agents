# Short-form and social writing

## Access boundary

The primary account is [@evancz on X/Twitter](https://x.com/evancz). A complete public export was not available during research. Search indexing returned an incomplete sample, and the accessible third-party profile mirror reported roughly 3,000 posts but did not provide a reliable, citable full archive. Deleted posts and replies may be absent. No claim of completeness is made.

## Observed sample

The accessible 2023–2026 sample included:

- compiler and npm release notices;
- requests for people to report differences before changing a release tag;
- thanks naming a contributor and the exact reliability improvement;
- links to talks with a one-question summary of the problem;
- follow-up sources from talks, including a searchable title when a link might be unstable;
- personal technical observations framed with uncertainty and an invitation for comparable experiences.

Public discovery sources: [indexed profile sample](https://mobile.twstalker.com/evancz), [Lambda Days’ preserved announcement context](https://mobile.twstalker.com/LambdaDays), and direct links in the [GitHub profile](https://github.com/evancz).

## Register synthesis

Short posts usually do one job. They lead with a practical fact or question, give just enough context to understand why the link matters, and make the feedback request specific. Announcements are modest rather than triumphant. Gratitude names concrete work. Uncertainty is explicit when the evidence is anecdotal.

Do not reproduce platform quirks or recurring phrases as a costume. Apply the information density, specificity, and feedback shape.
