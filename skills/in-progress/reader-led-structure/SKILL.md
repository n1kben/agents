# Reader-led structure

Find the reader's question and the answer the piece needs to earn. In one sentence, sketch the route from what the reader expects to what a specific case reveals and what follows. Use only details you can support.

- Start with the difficulty or decision. If a detail makes it surprising, show that detail early. Do not invent an anecdote or hide the answer for effect.
- Let the case show the key detail before stating the general rule. Explain the context or terms when the reader needs them.
- Make each paragraph pick up a question or complication raised by the last. Address a real objection where a reader would raise it.
- Stop when the answer or its consequence is clear. Do not add a recap to meet a word count.

When revising, look for claims you make more than once, even in different words. Keep the clearest version. Merge paragraphs that repeat the premise just to add one detail. Use headings and lists only when they help readers find their way.
